StepAlong

Your camera is the coach.

A movement-practice app that watches you through your own camera and tells you how you're doing. No wearables.

Why this exists: home movement practice for balance and mobility usually has no way to tell you if you're doing it right: no coach watching, no feedback, just guesswork. StepAlong uses your phone or laptop camera to watch your movement in real time and tell you how you're doing, wherever you are.

This is a working prototype. Swap this note for your own story before it's final.

StepAlong is a student-built prototype, not a medical device. Routines are inspired by published movement research but haven't been clinically tested. If you have a health condition, talk to a doctor or physical therapist before starting a new exercise routine.

Your camera stays private. Video is analyzed on your device while you practice. Nothing is uploaded, recorded, or saved.

Before you start

Set up your space

A minute of setup makes the tracking much more accurate and keeps practice safe.

  • 1
    Position the cameraProp your phone or laptop at about chest height so it can see you from head to toe.
  • 2
    Step back about 6 feet (2 m)Your whole body, feet included, should fit in the frame.
  • 3
    Light the roomFace a light source. Avoid a bright window behind you.
  • 4
    Make it safeClear the floor, wear non-slip footwear, and keep a sturdy chair or counter within reach. Stop if you feel dizzy or unwell.

Research behind this routine

How the scoring works

MediaPipe's pose model returns 33 body landmarks per frame. Each routine measures how far you move from your own resting position, scaled to your body size and smoothed to avoid jitter. Reaching the target size scores about 70, and a 100 needs you to go 20% beyond it. A rep counts when you hold at least 70 for the tier's hold time, and reps must alternate sides (left then right, heels then toes). Your round score is reps (40%), range (35%) and consistency (25%), so a 100 takes full-size, even reps at the target pace.
